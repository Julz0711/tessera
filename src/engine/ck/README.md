# Cities & Knights — rules as implemented

The engine follows the published Cities & Knights rules. Where the rulebook is ambiguous or where
an online game needs a precise answer, these are the interpretations used:

## Knights
- Recruit (1 wool + 1 ore) on an **empty intersection touching one of your roads**; knights ignore the distance rule. Each player owns 2 basic, 2 strong and 2 mighty knights.
- Activate for 1 grain. A knight **cannot move, displace or chase the robber on the turn it was activated** (Warlord counts as activation).
- Promote for 1 wool + 1 ore, once per knight per turn, only if a piece of the next level is in your supply. Mighty knights need Politics level 3 (Fortress).
- Moving travels along **your own roads**; it may pass your own pieces but not opponents' buildings or knights. Any action deactivates the knight.
- Displacing needs a strictly weaker opponent knight at the destination. The displaced knight's owner moves it (keeping level and active state) along their own roads to another empty intersection connected to the spot it was displaced from, passing their own pieces but not opponents'; with no such spot it is removed.
- Opponent knights block your roads and break your longest road, like settlements. No settlement may be built where a knight stands.

## Barbarians & robber
- The ship advances on each ship face of the event die; on the 7th step it attacks **before production**.
- Strength = number of cities on the board (metropolises included). Defense = sum of the levels of active knights.
- Island wins: the single strongest contributor gets a Defender VP; tied top contributors each draw a progress card from a deck of their choice. If nobody contributed, nobody is rewarded.
- Barbarians win: among players owning a city that isn't a metropolis, those with the lowest contribution each lose one city of their choice (auto-chosen if they have only one). A lost city keeps its settlement; its wall is lost.
- After every attack all knights become inactive and the ship returns to the start.
- Until the first attack a 7 still makes players discard, but the robber doesn't move; the Bishop and knight chases also wait for the first attack.

## Improvements & metropolises
- Level n of a track costs n commodities of that track (Trade: cloth, Politics: coin, Science: paper). You need at least one city to build improvements.
- Level-3 abilities: Trading House (commodities 2:1), Fortress (mighty knights), Aqueduct (a production roll that gives you nothing lets you take one resource; not on a 7).
- The first player to reach level 4 in a track gets that metropolis (+2 VP); a player reaching level 5 before the holder takes it; level 5 is permanent. You can't build a level that would win a metropolis without a city to put it on.
- Metropolises can't be lost to the barbarians.

## Progress cards
- Drawn when the event die shows a city gate and the red die is ≤ your level + 1 in that track (players draw in turn order starting with the roller).
- Hand limit: 4 (victory-point cards Printer and Constitution are revealed immediately and don't count).
- Played on your own turn after rolling, any number per turn — except the Alchemist, played before rolling. Played cards go to the bottom of their deck.
- Wedding: each player with more VP gives you 2 cards of their choice (fewer if they hold fewer).
- Saboteur: each opponent with at least your VP discards half their hand (rounded down).
- Commercial Harbor: you pick, per opponent, a resource to give; each opponent holding a commodity gives you one commodity of their choice.
- Master Merchant: look at the hand of a player with strictly more VP (public points; a tie is not enough) and take 2 cards.
- Spy: look at an opponent's progress cards and take one.
- Deserter: the victim removes one of their knights; you may place a knight of the same strength from your supply (or, with none left, the strongest weaker one), with the same status: active if the deserter was active. An active deserter counts as activated this turn, so it can't act until your next turn.
- Diplomat: remove an open road (one end connects to nothing of its owner's); if it was yours you may place it again immediately.
- Intrigue: displace an opponent's knight standing on an intersection touching your roads (no knight of yours needed). The intersection is left empty: its owner relocates the knight as for any displacement and can't put it back.
- Inventor: swap two number tokens other than 2, 12, 6 and 8.
- Merchant: place the merchant on a land hex next to your settlement or city — 2:1 trades for that hex's resource and +1 VP while you hold it.
- Merchant Fleet: 2:1 trades for one card type of your choice for the rest of the turn.
- Resource Monopoly: every opponent gives you up to 2 of the named resource. Trade Monopoly: up to 1 of the named commodity.
