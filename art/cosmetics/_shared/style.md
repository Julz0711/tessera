# Style guide

Shared research and rules for every cosmetic. Item briefs only add what is specific to their item.

## Where items are seen

- Lobby: characters stand in a row on a stage, full body, about 150 px tall, three-quarter front view. Each has a plinth under them, a companion beside them and their banner hanging above (reference: `docs/feedback/image-7.png`, but in our low-poly style).
- Profile and podium: same full body, larger.
- Portrait inside a surround: head and shoulders, 40 to 80 px. The face, hat and collar must read here.
- In game: banners fly on settlements, cities and knights at 20 to 30 px.

## Low-poly rules (from research)

- Silhouette first. With interior detail gone, the outline carries the item: exaggerate shapes (bigger hat, flared coat, oversized prop) rather than stay timid. Check every model as a black silhouette.
- Proportions: stylised heroic adult, about 6 heads tall, broad shoulders, slightly large hands and feet (reference: Synty POLYGON characters). The first Captain at 4.5 heads, built from stacked boxes, read as a nutcracker and was rejected.
- Spend triangles on the face, hat and the main prop; save on hands (mitten hand: one block plus thumb) and feet (boot blocks).
- Flat shading, one flat colour per face. No smoothing, no gradients, no image textures. The game already renders everything with flat-shaded `MeshStandardMaterial` (`src/components/board3d/materials.ts`), so models match the board.
- Small palette (below). Each model uses at most 8 palette colours plus `player`.
- Budgets: character 3,000 to 5,000 triangles per variant (the Captain is about 4,200), companion ≤ 1,500, plinth ≤ 1,500, die ≤ 300.
- Organic, continuous shapes: limbs taper into the torso, clothes have layers (lapels, cuffs, trims, jagged hems). Never stack primitives for bodies or heads.

## Player colour

Every character shows the player's colour on one large, front-facing area (a sash, scarf, apron or cloak lining) plus at most one small accent. It has to be visible from the front at 150 px, because it tells players apart in the lobby.

## Two variants per character

Every character comes in two variants the player picks between, so everyone can play someone like them: `a` (masculine build) and `b` (feminine build). Both wear the same outfit and carry the same props; they differ in body, face and hair. Vary age and skin tone between the two as well (Captain: a = older man, grey beard and queue, skin-2; b = woman in her 40s, dark hair framing the face and a braid down the back, skin-3). Variant b also gets narrower shoulders, a narrower waist, slimmer sleeves and her own head profile; the outfit scales to the build..

## How characters are built

Characters are generated by script, so a fix applies to both variants and survives every rebuild:

- `_shared/charlib.py`: shared building blocks. Skin-modifier body (one continuous mesh from a stick figure, subdivided and decimated for facets), ring-profile heads (`HEAD_A`, `HEAD_B`: cross-sections from chin to crown with brow ridge, eye sockets, cheekbones, jaw), grids fitted to the head for hair and beards, shells for coats and waistcoats, the rig, weights, posing and export.
- `characters/<id>/build.py`: only the character's own outfit, face details and pose. Run in Blender with `exec(open(".../build.py").read())`; it rebuilds both variants from scratch, then `review()` renders the previews.
- Weights: body automatic (heat); clothes copy the body's weights (data transfer), coat skirts blend to the hips below the waist; heads, hands and props are rigid to one bone.
- No clipping by construction: body faces hidden under clothes are deleted; the waistcoat only exists where it shows; coats keep 2 cm clearance over what is under them; necklines close onto the neck.
- Poses: `turn()` for spine and head, `reach()` solves an arm to a hand target (a fist lands on the coat, not inside it), `aim()` for legs. Weight on one leg, a slight turn, asymmetric arms.
- Rig bone names: `root, hips, spine, chest, neck, head, shoulder.L/R, upper_arm.L/R, forearm.L/R, hand.L/R, thigh.L/R, shin.L/R, foot.L/R`. Character faces -Y in Blender; left is +X. `idle`: 3 s loop keyed on the character's stance.
- Review: `py art/cosmetics/_shared/sheet.py <item folder>` puts both variants' renders, plus 150 px and 64 px checks, on one `review.png`. Check front, three-quarter and back for clipping before showing the user.

## Preview scene

`_shared/preview.blend` (collection `preview` plus its world): `cam_full` and `cam_portrait` (85 mm, three-quarter front, about 20° above), warm `key`, cool `rim` and soft `fill` sun lights, EEVEE, Standard view transform, transparent film. Append the collection into an item's file. Renders: `preview-<variant>.png` at 512 px (full body) and `portrait-<variant>.png` at 256 px (head and shoulders). Review on the lobby colour #2A211B, also scaled down to 150 px and 64 px.

## Export

glTF binary (`.glb`), Y up, modifiers applied, animations included. Origin at the feet (characters, companions) or the base centre (plinths, dice).

- Join all parts into one mesh per variant before exporting (45 separate parts cost 3 times the file size).
- Export without normals and texture coordinates (`export_normals=False`, `export_texcoords=False`): the glTF spec makes viewers compute flat normals then, which is our look anyway, and vertices can be shared. This took the Captain from 155 KB to 65 KB.
- Characters export one file per variant: `<id>-a.glb`, `<id>-b.glb`, so a player only downloads the one they wear.
- Target under 150 KB per file (the Captain is about 132 KB at 4,200 triangles). Meshopt compression can halve that later if needed.
